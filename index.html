Your questions

Your Stripe questions, answered

From your WhatsApp on 29 Sep.

Will my home address show on the Playbook invoice?

No. The Playbook is sold through Stripe’s Managed Payments, so Link (Stripe’s company) is the seller. The receipt and the invoice come from Link, with Link’s address: the South San Francisco one you saw. And now that your address is gone from Stripe’s public details, it isn’t shown anywhere else either.

Should the Playbook checkout collect names and addresses?

Take the address boxes off: untick “Collect customer addresses”. Stripe still asks US buyers for the full address it needs for tax, and everyone else gets a shorter checkout, so more people finish paying. Names can go too, because the card form already asks for the name on the card. Keeping names is fine if you prefer.

Should I turn receipts on for payments and refunds?

Yes: Settings → Customer emails → “Successful payments” and “Refunds”. They’re free, and with no address in your public details, none shows on them. Your 1:1 thank-you page tells clients a receipt is on its way, so this keeps that true. Playbook buyers get Link’s receipt either way. Leave the post-payment invoices (0.4%) off: receipts are enough.

For 1:1 I collect names but not addresses. Is that OK?

Yes. Name and email are all you need for 1:1 sessions.

Launch price: leave it for now and make the higher price later?

Yes. The website keeps saying “launch price”, and you make the £39.99 link only when the launch price ends. Tell Diogo that day and the website switches over at the same time.

ManyChat: didn’t I already choose Pro?

You did pick Pro, but the account still says “Trial”. Have a look in Settings → Billing: if Pro is chosen and a card is on file, it carries on by itself when the trial ends.

When do I pay for Kit’s Creator plan?

Not yet. Only the three welcome emails need it. Diogo will tell you the day, just before launch, so there’s time to load and test them.

Why two DMs and not the link straight away?

Instagram lets a business send only one automatic message to someone who comments, until that person replies. Tapping the button counts as their reply, so the second message with the link can go out, and the chat stays open for them to message you back.

3 · Payments and VAT

Selling the Playbook, and the e-book VAT setting

You’ve already made the Stripe link with Managed Payments switched on. Here’s what that does, where the £5 went, and the one setting that fixes it.

What Managed Payments does

Normally you’re the seller, so every tax in every country is your problem. With Managed Payments switched on, Stripe becomes the legal seller of the Playbook. For each buyer it works out the right tax for their country, charges it and pays it to that country. It also deals with fraud, chargebacks and payment questions. You get what’s left. The cost is 3.5% per sale, on top of Stripe’s normal card fee.

Why you need it, even under £90,000

The £90,000 threshold is only about UK VAT. When a UK seller sells a digital product to someone in the EU, EU VAT is due from the very first sale, with no threshold, and other countries have their own rules. Most of your audience is outside the UK. Without Managed Payments you’d have to register for EU VAT and file a return every three months, even in months with no sales.

Where the £5 went

Because Stripe is the seller, it charges the VAT that applies to the type of product you choose in Stripe. If the Playbook is set as a general “digital service”, a UK buyer is charged 20% VAT. Your price is fixed at £29.99 including tax, so that 20%, which is £5.00, comes out of your £29.99. That’s almost certainly the £5 you saw: it isn’t a Stripe fee, it’s UK VAT. But e-books have had 0% VAT in the UK since May 2020. Set the Playbook as an e-book and UK buyers pay no VAT, so you keep that £5 on every UK sale.

How to set it (about 5 minutes)

  1. In Stripe, open Product catalogue and click the Playbook.
  2. Edit the tax category (sometimes called the tax code) and choose: Digital Books – downloaded – non subscription – with permanent rights
  3. Check the price is set to include tax, so buyers always pay exactly £29.99.
  4. Test it: open the payment link and type in a UK address. The VAT line should say £0.00.
  5. Then try a German address. A small VAT amount should appear, which is correct.

Stripe’s menu names can change slightly; the category name is the part that matters.

Good to know about Managed Payments

  • Buyers see “Link” (Stripe’s company) as the seller at checkout, and LINK.COM* on their bank statement.
  • Stripe sends the receipt. We can’t add our own text to the checkout page.
  • Stripe may refund a buyer within 60 days in some cases (for example to stop a chargeback), and may refund if a customer’s question gets no answer within 48 hours. Keep an eye on emails from Stripe.
  • Changing the price means making a new payment link. The website can switch its buttons over to the new link automatically.
