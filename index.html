Naming the free guide · 4 Oct

You and Diogo agreed the free guide needs a new name: “The Sports Physio Career Roadmap” sounds like “The Sports Physio Career Playbook”, so the free one competes with the one you sell. Below is our thinking, lots of names to pick from, and a few quick questions. Star every name you like, then send your picks to Diogo.

The idea

What the free guide is for

The free guide's job

Get as many of the right people as possible to sign up. After that, the emails do the selling: first the Playbook, later 1:1.

Why the names compete

“Roadmap” and “Playbook” both sound like the complete answer, and both start with “The Sports Physio Career”. People can't tell which one is the starter and which is the full thing.

How to stop it

Free guide = the five things that matter. Playbook = how to do each one, step by step. 1:1 = your own plan. If the names say that, they help each other instead of competing.

Who it’s for

Students, or everyone who wants sport?

Points to students
  • The free guide was written as “what I wish I'd understood at university”, and 589 people signed up for it on your Google Form.
  • A third of those sign-ups are in India, Egypt, Pakistan and Nigeria, where there are lots of young students.
  • “I'm a student” and “too expensive” are common replies in your DMs, and TikTok audiences skew young.
Points to graduates and qualified physios
  • Your best post, “You've got the physio degree”, speaks to graduates: 920 likes, 417 comments, 376 saves.
  • 1:1 sessions at £70 booked out fast (12 in two days from Stories). That's people with an income.
  • When we read your comments for the funnel plan, the people likely to pay looked like qualified physios.

Our honest view: it's probably a mix. Students bring the numbers; graduates and qualified physios bring the money. Nothing records the split yet, so a name about university could turn away the people most likely to buy the Playbook and 1:1. We'd name the free guide after the goal everyone shares, getting into sport, and keep your university line inside the guide as your story.

A good name

Seven rules from the best copywriters

  1. Look different from the PlaybookNo “The Sports Physio Career …”.
  2. Promise what matters, not the howThe how is the Playbook's job.
  3. Use a number“5” makes it feel quick, so it reads as the starter. (Halbert: specifics sell.)
  4. Use their words and their painDegree, getting into sport, nobody tells you. (Collier: join the conversation already in their head.)
  5. Clear before cleverIt must make sense in a DM and on a small cover. (Ogilvy, Caples: self-interest first, then curiosity.)
  6. Fit everyone who wants sportStudents, new graduates and qualified physios. (Schwartz: meet them where they are.)
  7. Never promise a jobYour page already says it: “Not a job. A plan.”

3 · Payments and VAT

Selling the Playbook, and the e-book VAT setting

You’ve already made the Stripe link with Managed Payments switched on. Here’s what that does, where the £5 went, and the one setting that fixes it.

What Managed Payments does

Normally you’re the seller, so every tax in every country is your problem. With Managed Payments switched on, Stripe becomes the legal seller of the Playbook. For each buyer it works out the right tax for their country, charges it and pays it to that country. It also deals with fraud, chargebacks and payment questions. You get what’s left. The cost is 3.5% per sale, on top of Stripe’s normal card fee.

Why you need it, even under £90,000

The £90,000 threshold is only about UK VAT. When a UK seller sells a digital product to someone in the EU, EU VAT is due from the very first sale, with no threshold, and other countries have their own rules. Most of your audience is outside the UK. Without Managed Payments you’d have to register for EU VAT and file a return every three months, even in months with no sales.

Where the £5 went

Because Stripe is the seller, it charges the VAT that applies to the type of product you choose in Stripe. If the Playbook is set as a general “digital service”, a UK buyer is charged 20% VAT. Your price is fixed at £29.99 including tax, so that 20%, which is £5.00, comes out of your £29.99. That’s almost certainly the £5 you saw: it isn’t a Stripe fee, it’s UK VAT. But e-books have had 0% VAT in the UK since May 2020. Set the Playbook as an e-book and UK buyers pay no VAT, so you keep that £5 on every UK sale.

  4. Test it: open the payment link and type in a UK address. The VAT line should say £0.00.

Good to know about Managed Payments

  • Buyers see “Link” (Stripe’s company) as the seller at checkout, and LINK.COM* on their bank statement.
  • Stripe sends the receipt. We can’t add our own text to the checkout page.
  • Stripe may refund a buyer within 60 days in some cases (for example to stop a chargeback), and may refund if a customer’s question gets no answer within 48 hours. Keep an eye on emails from Stripe.
  • Changing the price means making a new payment link. The website can switch its buttons over to the new link automatically.
